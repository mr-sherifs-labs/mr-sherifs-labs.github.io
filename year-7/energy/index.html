Challenge card

Write your answers in your copybook. Predict first, then use the lab to check.

Questions

  1. Lift the ball to 10 m and hold it there. Which energy store is filling up? Which tank shows it?
  2. Lift the light ball to 10 m, then the heavy one. Which has more gravitational potential energy? Why?
  3. Drop the ball. As it falls, which store empties and which store fills? Look at the Total tank: what happens to it?
  4. Watch the ball bounce. Why is each bounce lower than the one before? Where does the energy go?
  5. Drop the clay. Why does it not bounce at all? Name two stores or transfers the energy ends up as.
  6. Drop the parachute. Why does it fall so slowly? Which tank fills up while it is still falling?
  7. After everything has stopped moving, has any energy been destroyed? Use the word dissipated in your answer.

How to use

  1. Drag the object up the ruler (touch anywhere on the sheet and slide up or down), then let go.
  2. Or press Lift to 10 m, then Drop it.
  3. The coloured liquid inside the object, and the tanks beside it, show how much energy is in each store.

How the model works: gravitational potential energy is mass × 10 N/kg × height, and kinetic energy comes from the speed. At each bounce a set fraction of the speed is kept; whatever kinetic energy is lost is shared between thermal energy (most) and sound. Air resistance turns kinetic energy into thermal energy while the object moves.

How to use

  2. Tap an energy card, then tap a gap to put it there. You can also drag a card into a gap.
  3. Tap a filled gap to empty it. Press Check my chain when every gap is full.
  4. The words on the arrows (like "burning") tell you what is changing the energy.

The situations follow the energy stores and transfers used in your lessons: chemical, elastic potential, gravitational potential, kinetic and thermal stores; electrical, sound, light and thermal transfers.

Challenge card

Copy the readings table into your copybook, plot the points, and draw a smooth curve. Never join the dots with straight lines.

Questions

  1. Run one cup with no lid. When does the tea cool fastest: at the start or near the end? How can you tell from the curve?
  2. What temperature does the tea end up at? Why does it stop cooling there?
  3. Watch the thermometer next to the cup. What happens to it? What does this prove about where the thermal energy went?
  4. Press Keep this line, then run a cup with the lid on. Which cools faster? Name two things you must keep the same to make it a fair test.
  5. Find how much the temperature fell in the first 3 minutes (180 s). Work it out as a percentage of the starting temperature.
  6. Describe how you would prove in a real lab that thermal energy is dissipated from a cup of tea. (3 marks)

How to use

  1. Choose the cup and the room, then press Start. Time runs faster than real life (see Speed).
  2. A reading is taken every 60 seconds and marked with a cross on the graph and in the table.
  3. The red dots are thermal energy. Watch them leave the tea and spread out into the room.

How the model works: the tea loses thermal energy faster the hotter it is compared with the room (Newton's law of cooling). A lid, an insulated cup or more tea slow it down; a fan speeds it up. The thermometer beside the cup warms slightly as energy flows out of the tea.
